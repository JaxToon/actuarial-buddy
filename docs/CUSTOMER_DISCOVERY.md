# Customer Discovery — No Existing Industry Network

## Goal

Find out whether a specific actuarial workflow is painful enough that a practitioner would test and potentially pay for a small tool. This activity is research, not sales pitching.

## 1. Who to contact

Search for people by role, not only by company:

- Pricing Actuary / Pricing Analyst.
- Group Medical / Employee Benefits Actuary.
- Valuation Actuary / Reserving Actuary.
- Actuarial Analyst / Senior Actuarial Analyst.
- Actuarial Consultant / Manager — Actuarial.
- Head of Actuarial / Chief Actuary.
- Product Pricing / Underwriting manager for health products.

Potential organizations to map from public sources:

- Life and health insurers and reinsurers listed in OJK publications/directories.
- Actuarial and insurance consulting practices.
- Third-party administrators (TPAs) and employee-benefit brokers only as adjacent stakeholders, not assumed buyers.
- University actuarial programs, actuarial student clubs and alumni communities.
- PAI public CPD/events and professional activities.

Do not assume that a named company has a need or that a job title means the individual is a decision-maker.

## 2. Where to find people without personal introductions

1. LinkedIn: search exact job titles plus Indonesia/Jakarta; check whether the person publicly welcomes professional discussion.
2. PAI: review its public events, CPD and professional activities; attend an event or ask the organizer about public learning/discussion channels.
3. University networks: actuarial mathematics/statistics lecturers, student associations, alumni and final-year students working in insurance.
4. Public job posts: use them to identify recurring roles, skills and business functions; do not scrape private contact details.
5. Insurance conferences, webinars and public OJK/industry forums.
6. Actuarial consulting firms' public contact forms and official business channels.

A reasonable first target is 15–20 thoughtful outreach attempts to obtain 5–8 short conversations. These are suggested activity targets, not expected conversion rates.

## 5. Interview structure (20–25 minutes)

### A. Context (3 minutes)

- Apa peran Bapak/Ibu dan pekerjaan aktuaria apa yang paling sering ditangani?
- Apakah pekerjaan yang dibahas untuk asuransi jiwa, kesehatan, umum, atau beberapa lini?

### B. Recent real workflow (8 minutes)

- Ceritakan studi pengalaman atau renewal pricing terakhir yang dikerjakan dari awal sampai report selesai.
- File/data apa saja yang dibutuhkan? Siapa yang menyiapkannya?
- Bagaimana exposure, claim count, incurred claims, dan premium didefinisikan?
- Bagaimana menangani klaim terlambat, outstanding claim, large claims, dan perubahan manfaat?
- Bagian mana yang paling sering perlu dicek atau dikerjakan ulang?

### C. Current tools and controls (5 minutes)

- Tools apa yang digunakan (Excel, R, Python, SAS, Prophet, sistem internal, lainnya)?
- Apakah asumsi diberi versi dan approval? Bagaimana membandingkan periode sebelumnya?
- Jika angka berubah, bagaimana reviewer mengetahui penyebabnya?
- Format report dan workbook apa yang wajib ada?

### D. Prototype reaction (5 minutes)

Show only a synthetic demo. Ask:

- Bagian mana yang berguna dan bagian mana yang tidak relevan?
- Apa yang salah atau hilang dari definisi metrik/flow ini?
- Apakah tool ini harus berjalan lokal, private cloud, on-premise atau boleh SaaS?
- Apa syarat minimal agar tim bersedia menguji dengan data sintetis/de-identified?

### E. Buying process (3 minutes)

- Siapa yang biasanya memilih dan menyetujui software seperti ini?
- Apakah kebutuhan ini punya anggaran tersendiri atau harus masuk proyek IT/actuarial transformation?
- Apa yang harus dibuktikan sebelum vendor baru boleh menjalani pilot?

## 6. Interview note template

For each conversation, record only information the person consents to share:

- Date and role (company optional unless they agree).
- Line of business and workflow.
- Current tools and process steps.
- Three biggest pain points, ranked by severity/frequency.
- Data fields and grain needed (no actual customer records).
- Required controls and report outputs.
- Security/deployment constraints.
- Reaction to synthetic demo.
- Buying process and likely budget owner.
- Exact phrases/quotes only with permission.
- Evidence strength: observed workflow / opinion / assumption.
- Follow-up action.

## 7. Decision rule after interviews

Continue the group-health wedge if:

- at least 3 practitioners independently describe a similar data preparation or experience analysis problem;
- at least 2 can review the workflow or calculation definitions;
- at least 1 is willing to test a synthetic/de-identified prototype; and
- the workflow is not already solved adequately by their current platform.

Re-scope if most interviewees care more about claims development, IBNR, renewal reporting, mortality assumptions or PSAK 117 reconciliation than group-health rate indication. Do not add all those modules at once; pick the next repeated problem.

## 8. Do not do these things

- Do not ask for policyholder datasets, health records, diagnosis data, NIK or internal models during discovery.
- Do not ask a practitioner to disclose confidential model points, assumptions, client names or proprietary tools.
- Do not present synthetic outputs as real market experience.
- Do not say the prototype is compliant or certified.
- Do not interpret polite interest as willingness to buy. Ask about the actual approval and budget process.
